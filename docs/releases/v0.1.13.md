## Summary

`parakeet-cli` v0.1.13 reports a pause when it happens. v0.1.12 measured silence on every 32 ms chunk but reported it only on the 0.5 s preview cadence, so a client waiting for 300 ms of silence heard about it up to 500 ms late, and the words before the pause were decoded just as late. A session that streams partials now sends a tick as soon as a pause is 128 ms old, decodes the words before it right then, and says on every tick how much audio the text reflects. Final transcripts and protocol 2 are unchanged.

## What's Changed

- When the silence after speech reaches 128 ms, the session sends a `tick` at once. If at least 0.25 s of audio has arrived since the last preview, it also takes a preview, so the last words before the pause are decoded then instead of at the next cadence tick. The 0.5 s cadence restarts from that preview.
- While the pause lasts, a `tick` follows for every further 64 ms of silence, up to 1.6 s. These carry no preview. Past 1.6 s only the cadence ticks remain, so an idle open microphone still sends two ticks a second.
- Silence before the first word of a session is not a pause and sends no extra ticks.
- Every `tick` has a new `decoded_ms` field: the `audio_ms` of the newest finished preview decode, whether or not its text changed. Once it reaches `audio_ms - trailing_silence_ms`, the latest `partial` is the text of everything said so far. A tick that reports a decode always follows that decode's `partial`.
- The single preview slot now keeps the newest preview when the recognizer is busy. It used to keep the oldest, so the decode after a slow one could be of audio one tick old.
- `transcribe --session --partials` prints the same ticks.

## Integration Migration

No change is required. Clients that ignore unknown fields keep working and receive more ticks during a pause. A client that acts on interim text when a tick reports silence should also check `decoded_ms`, or it can act on text that has not heard the last words.

## Validation

- 78 Rust tests pass, including the pause cadence, the 0.25 s preview gap, the 1.6 s window, the lead-in silence, the `decoded_ms` wire value, and the newest-preview slot
- replaying a three-sentence command at 12 start offsets (0 to 495 ms), and counting a preview as decoded 150 ms after it was taken:
  - a tick with at least 300 ms of silence and current text comes 306 to 336 ms after the last word, against 294 to 708 ms (median 469) for v0.1.12's first 300 ms tick with the last word on screen
  - a tick of at least 900 ms comes 936 to 966 ms after the last word, against 900 to 1378 ms (median 1122)
  - the session takes the same number of previews, about 22 for the 11 s recording, because a pause preview restarts the cadence. It sends about 52 ticks, against 22
  - the final transcript is identical to v0.1.12 at every offset, and for the 48 s demo recording
- the replay is offline and the 150 ms decode time is a model, not a measurement of this change. It has not been run through a microphone
- interim `partial` text can differ from v0.1.12, because a pause now takes a preview and restarts the cadence
