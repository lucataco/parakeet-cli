## Summary

`parakeet-cli` v0.1.9 streams interim text more often: a `partial` every 0.5 s of captured audio instead of every 0.75 s, and each partial now says how much audio it covers (`audio_ms`). Voice-command clients such as Superkeet act on interim text while the user is still speaking, so a shorter cadence directly shortens the time between saying something and it happening. Protocol 2 is unchanged; `audio_ms` is an additive field.

## What's Changed

- `PREVIEW_INTERVAL_SAMPLES` is 8 000 samples (0.5 s at 16 kHz), down from 12 000; `PREVIEW_MIN_SAMPLES` stays 0.4 s
- `partial` events carry `audio_ms`, the session audio received when the preview was taken; `Segmenter` counts received samples and `Preview` reports them
- `parakeet transcribe --session --partials` prints `audio_ms` too, so a replay can be lined up with the recording without a microphone
- committed segments, the final `complete` text, loss accounting and the 15 s preview cap are unchanged

## Integration Migration

No change is required. Clients that ignore unknown fields keep working. A client that ends an utterance on "interim text unchanged for N seconds" can use a shorter N, since the engine now reports progress every 0.5 s.

## Cost

Each preview re-encodes the uncommitted tail (about 30 ms plus 14 ms per second of tail on Apple Silicon CPU, capped at ~250 ms by the 15 s window). At the shorter cadence a partials session does about 50% more preview work; sessions without `partials` do no extra work.

## Validation

- Rust tests pass, including the cadence tick count, preview window, and `audio_ms` on emitted partials
- replaying Superkeet's 48 s demo recording take by take yields partials every ~510 ms of audio
